# Figure and data interpretation

Use OBSERVE → QUANTIFY → INTERPRET → CONNECT → LIMIT as a reasoning aid, not mandatory output headings.

## Establish what is available

Identify the sample, time, exposure condition, units, axes, normalization, and whether the source contains measurements, processed measurements, fit outputs, predictions, or a mixture. Read only relevant project facts and figures. Do not infer the experimental system from a workflow example.

Establish the supporting observation before assigning a cause. This is an evidence-order rule within an argument, not an instruction to describe every figure before explaining any of them. Observations and bounded interpretations can appear together. When manuscript prose is requested, use Writing to choose their placement for the section type. Prefer available numerical data to visual estimates. If only a plot is available, use qualitative trends unless approximate digitization is requested and appropriate; label estimates and uncertainty. Never turn an image impression into an exact numerical result.

Comparisons require compatible conditions, units, normalization, definitions, and denominators. Distinguish replicate variability, instrument uncertainty, fitting uncertainty, and prediction error when the evidence permits. Do not invent replicates, error bars, or statistical significance.

## Bound interpretations

- EIS and equivalent-circuit fitting: distinguish measured impedance from fitted parameters. Verify the circuit, parameter definitions, units, fit quality, and frequency of comparison before interpretation. A fitted resistance or low-frequency impedance alone does not uniquely identify corrosion chemistry or directly supply corrosion rate.
- SEM/EDS: morphology and elemental distribution can support local changes; elemental detection alone is insufficient for unique phase or oxidation-state assignment.
- Raman/XPS: inspect assignments, overlapping features, measurement/processing conditions, and supporting references before concluding phase or chemical-state changes. A single ambiguous feature does not establish an entire transformation pathway.
- Model prediction: distinguish measured from predicted time evolution. Prediction agreement can assess predictive performance; it is not independent experimental validation of a mechanism. Data-derived fits and their input data are not independent evidence merely because displayed separately.
- Model comparisons: confirm train/validation/test separation, time or sample grouping, preprocessing fit scope, prediction horizon, metric definitions/scale, and comparable evaluation sets. Flag leakage or missing evaluation information rather than inferring superior performance from an isolated metric.
- Electromagnetic measurements: distinguish electromagnetic impedance matching and absorption metrics from low-frequency electrochemical impedance and corrosion protection.

Use the above branches only for techniques present in the current task. They are interpretation checks, not evidence that the project actually used these methods.

## Connect and limit

Align samples, stages, and exposure conditions before combining techniques. State what each adds and whether it is independent. Describe a mechanism as an interpretation supported by multiple observations when warranted; do not promote a model output or one technique into proof of the complete process.

Read established project mechanisms or background only for tasks to which they are relevant. Treat it as user-established project background with its stated provenance; do not claim that you independently revalidated all underlying data. Surface conflicting project data outside the draft for reconciliation.

Deliver the trend, supported quantitative comparisons, bounded interpretation, and material limitations. If manuscript prose is requested, pass this evidence into the writing module and honor the project's pre-drafting literature requirements.
