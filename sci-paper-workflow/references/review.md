# Scientific review

Review the requested scope. A request to review does not itself request a rewrite. For a paragraph review, load neighboring material only for an actual reference, term, or logical dependency; a manuscript-wide consistency check can justify broader reading.

Prioritize consequential issues:

1. Unsupported or contradictory science, measurement/prediction confusion, invented or mismatched references, and numerical errors.
2. Missing evidence links, applicability gaps, excessive mechanistic certainty, and unfair comparisons.
3. Inconsistent sample/time/units/terminology, figure or citation references, and argument structure.
4. Language, repetition, and readability.

This order is a priority guide, not a requirement to generate findings in every category. Identify the actual passage and evidence for each material finding. Distinguish demonstrated errors from unresolved questions or preferences.

For section-level writing review, also read the prose continuously without the source map. Assess whether its organization fits its scientific task and section type, interpretations are close enough to their supporting results, successive techniques add information, and the conclusion uses evidence already introduced. Identify repeated trend narration or unnecessary returns to earlier stages by location. Do not penalize descriptive Results for deferring interpretation to a separate Discussion, impose one outline on every section, or equate complete provenance records with coherent prose. Local edits retain their original scope.

Use a compact finding format: severity → location → problem → evidence or verification gap → smallest useful correction. Suggested severities: major (changes the scientific conclusion or validity), moderate (weakens support or clarity materially), minor (expression without scientific change).

Invoke literature or figure/data verification only for affected claims and missing evidence. Do not treat a cached “verified paper” label as verification of every claim in the paragraph. Review abstract-only citations according to their actual support and disclose the limitation.

If revision is requested, correct the identified scope and necessary linked references, then recheck those changes. If review only is requested, provide findings and proposed corrections without editing the manuscript. Report no material findings when that is the result; do not manufacture criticisms to fill a template.
