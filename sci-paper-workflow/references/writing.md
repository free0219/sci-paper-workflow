# Writing: section organization and literature-guided paragraphs

## Scope

Determine whether the task is new drafting, scientific revision, restructuring, language editing, translation, or compression. Identify the current section's purpose and use only relevant requirements, data, background, terminology, and necessary neighboring context. Do not automatically inherit another section's wording, argumentative structure, citations, or conclusions.

Preserve the requested revision scope. Explain necessary linked changes before making them; do not turn a local edit into a section rewrite. A request for wording-only revision or faithful translation does not trigger a new full-section exemplar search. Verify consequential factual issues in the affected scope according to project requirements.

For new drafting or authorized argumentative restructuring, actively seek a suitable original-paper passage for each substantive paragraph. Search relevant available local sources and the web, following the user's access constraints and journal preferences. Prioritize directly relevant peer-reviewed original research in the user's field. When a preferred journal lacks adequate evidence or exemplars, expand to other appropriate journals; do not impose a fixed journal across projects.

## Two kinds of provenance

Track these roles separately:

- Argument exemplar: an original passage whose scientific function, evidence sequence, inference structure, and conclusion boundaries are useful for organizing this paragraph.
- Content evidence: verified literature or project data that support this paragraph's actual scientific statements.

A source may serve either or both roles. Structural fit does not establish evidential support, and topical relevance alone does not establish structural fit. Another study's evidence cannot substitute for missing project results.

## 1. Establish the section's task before fixing paragraphs

Identify what the reader should understand by the end of this section, the evidence available, and the explanations reserved for later sections. Establish this from the current request and necessary project context even in a fresh task; independence from old drafts does not remove the need for a scientific purpose. Do not import an old outline or require the user to approve routine structural decisions.

Identify the section type and its purpose, such as an Introduction establishing a gap, Methods enabling reproduction, Results reporting observations, combined Results and Discussion, or a dedicated synthesis. For results-bearing sections, keep candidate organizing principles open, such as method, stage, comparison, or scientific question. Do not fix a sequence of separate description and explanation paragraphs before examining suitable literature. Respect an organization explicitly requested by the user.

## 2. Request both evidence and passages from Literature

Give the Literature module a concise retrieval brief containing:

- The section's scientific task, candidate organizing principles, and intended claims; refine paragraph functions after comparing structures.
- Relevant material/system, conditions, technique, and evidence type, only where known.
- The required passage role, such as framing a problem, comparing methods, interpreting observations, or delimiting a research gap.
- Project journal preferences, known aliases, and relevant cached sources.

For a new section or authorized section restructuring, request relevant complete subsections or connected passages as well as individual claim evidence. Inspect how the source connects observations, interpretations, and successive techniques, rather than choosing isolated paragraphs solely by topic. A local revision needs only the affected passage and necessary neighbors. Use Literature's existing retrieval workflow; titles and abstracts alone cannot establish a writing exemplar.

Read the actual candidate passage and enough neighboring text, figures, or methods to understand its argumentative role and limits. Record paper identity and a retrievable location: section, PDF file page (1-based, separate from printed numbering), and paragraph opening cue; for authoritative HTML full text, use heading and a short paragraph anchor. Do not invent a page or claim to have inspected inaccessible text.

## 3. Compare and select

First choose the section organization from the inspected sources and this study's evidence. Assess where the main finding becomes clear, how close explanations sit to their supporting observations, what each technique adds, and which conclusions need cross-technique synthesis. An opening trend statement can orient an evolution analysis; it is not mandatory for every section. Neither journal prestige nor the availability of a paragraph exemplar makes a whole outline suitable.

When multiple useful candidates exist, compare their scientific function, relevance, evidence requirements, inference strength, and fit with the surrounding manuscript. Select a primary exemplar or a small set of complementary passages. Briefly record why the selected approach fits; do not produce a long ranking or meet an arbitrary paper quota.

Abstract the useful argumentative moves: what the passage establishes first, what evidence follows, how that evidence is interpreted, and how the paragraph closes or connects forward. Do not extract a sentence-by-sentence imitation template.

Then define argument units: linked observations and the question, interpretation, or conclusion they address. A unit may occupy one or several adjacent paragraphs; it is not synonymous with a figure panel or a source paper. Select paragraph exemplars within this organization and keep boundaries revisable. One paper may support several paragraphs if each relevant passage and use are identified. Reassess cached exemplars rather than importing a previous section's outline automatically.

## 4. Map before drafting

Record the chosen section sequence, inspected structural reference locations, and a brief reason it fits. Then keep a compact map that retains paragraph-level traceability:

| Argument unit / paragraph(s) | Scientific function and connection to next unit | Exemplar and original location | Adapted argument sequence | Actual claim/data evidence | Necessary adaptation or gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Populate it with information actually inspected; it is not a prompt to invent missing sources. Keep it in task notes or a compact table, not a separate file per paragraph. It is an auditable writing plan, not a requirement to reveal a private reasoning transcript or obtain approval before drafting.

Build the adapted sequence using this study's facts and verified sources. Omit or change a source's argumentative step when this study lacks the required evidence. Do not reverse the workflow by writing generic prose first and retrospectively assigning loosely related exemplar papers.

## 5. Write and translate

### Lead with findings; consolidate boundaries

Distinguish analysis with the user from manuscript prose. In analysis, explicitly discuss limitations, counterevidence, unsupported inferences, and remaining uncertainty when useful for judgment. Do not automatically transfer that defensive framing into the manuscript.

In manuscript prose, including paragraph conclusions, section summaries, and the article's Conclusions, foreground the substantive finding, demonstrated capability, or scientific contribution supported by the evidence. Avoid defensive conclusions built around "cannot prove", "does not imply", "only demonstrates", or "further verification is required" as routine closing formulas. Positive framing means clearly stating what the evidence establishes; it does not require favorable results or conceal negative findings.

Develop the main result and its significance first. Consolidate broader applicability limits, unresolved mechanisms, generalization restrictions, and future validation needs in the designated limitations or closing discussion. Do not repeat those boundaries after each result or append a caution to every positive conclusion. In a scoped subsection edit, leave the planned boundary discussion in its designated location; flag any missing coverage outside the manuscript rather than rewriting unrelated sections.

Keep a concise local qualification only when omitting it would make the claim factually wrong or materially misleading, such as the comparison baseline, evaluated conditions, or fitted rather than measured status of a quantity. Prefer precise scope within an affirmative statement to an added defensive sentence. Do not postpone contradictory results that change the main finding or use a later limitations section to excuse an unsupported claim.

### Compose and translate

In combined Results and Discussion, normally keep a key observation, useful quantification, and its interpretation in the same or adjacent paragraphs. Include local qualifications essential to accuracy as described above; consolidate broader boundaries in their designated discussion. Distinguish measured facts from hypotheses through wording and evidence, not by postponing all explanations until every figure has been described. Each successive technique should add evidence, resolve a question, or provide a justified cross-check rather than restart the entire trend narrative. Reserve conclusions requiring several techniques for the appropriate synthesis.

This proximity principle is conditional: descriptive Results may defer interpretation to a separate Discussion, and a cross-technique argument may need several observations before an explanation is warranted. Choose the arrangement that fits the section's function and available evidence; do not force all work into a summary-then-methods template. Keep necessary qualifications close to the affected claim without ending every paragraph with the same promise of later verification.

Draft the paragraph in the requested manuscript language (English when unspecified) independently from the adapted argument and actual evidence. Borrow useful scientific organization and standard terminology, not distinctive wording, sentence skeletons, or translated versions of the original passage. Avoid patchwriting and do not reproduce unnecessary source excerpts.

Attribute borrowed scientific ideas, methods, and findings appropriately. A source used only for general rhetorical organization belongs in the outside-text exemplar note; do not insert it as an evidentiary citation for an unrelated claim. Use additional evidence sources wherever needed.

Use precise, restrained expression. Do not increase complexity merely to sound academic. Do not infer a universal research gap from a limited search, invent Methods conditions, or introduce new conclusions to strengthen an ending. Keep scientific claims and uncertainty within what the data and literature support.

After the manuscript content is settled, produce the requested translation if required. Preserve technical meaning, numerical values, causal direction, qualifications, uncertainty, and citation relationships. Translation must not add conclusions absent from the source-language draft.

## 6. Check and deliver

For each substantive paragraph, verify:

- The stated exemplar was actually read at the recorded location and fits the paragraph's function.
- The prose follows an appropriate adapted scientific argument rather than merely containing related citations.
- Each important claim is supported by the correct source or project data; rhetorical similarity has not been mistaken for scientific support.
- The wording is original, logical transitions are justified, and no evidence was invented to fit a template.
- Language, terminology, English/Chinese correspondence, and requested revision boundaries remain correct.
- Paragraph and section conclusions communicate the supported finding or contribution rather than defaulting to defensive caveats; broader boundaries are covered in the designated discussion without repetitive disclaimers, and essential local scope remains accurate.

Then read the authorized scope continuously, without its provenance table. Check whether the chosen organization serves its scientific task, whether explanations are unnecessarily distant from their evidence, whether returning to earlier stages merely repeats observations, and whether each unit advances the argument. Check that the closing conclusion follows from evidence already introduced rather than treating internal project background or later characterization as an established result. A complete source map does not by itself pass this coherence check. Fix material problems within the authorized scope before delivery.

For new-section delivery, follow the requested language and order. If no language preference is stated, provide an English draft followed by actual references and their supported claims. When English with Chinese translation is requested, use English → Chinese → actual references and their supported claims. Then provide a concise note covering the section organization and paragraph exemplars, with locations and material limitations; combine it with the reference table when clearer. Keep literature differences outside the manuscript unless the user requests discussion or factual accuracy requires qualification.

For local edits, return only the requested revised scope and a brief change description. For review-only requests, use the Review module without rewriting the manuscript.

## Fit, exceptions, and reuse

Seek exemplars for substantive paragraphs, especially argumentative Introduction and Discussion passages. Methods, descriptive Results, Abstracts, and Conclusions may also use functionally similar passages, but their reported work and findings remain this study's own. Do not force citations into every paragraph or require distinct papers for each paragraph. Short linking sentences can be covered by their parent paragraph.

If focused retrieval yields no adequately matched section or passage, state the specific gap and search boundary outside the manuscript. Continue a defensible organization and paragraph from verified data and the section's scientific task, without claiming unavailable exemplar-based provenance. Do not force a weakly related paper into the map merely to fill a row. If the scientific evidence itself is insufficient, withhold or qualify the unsupported claim as appropriate.

For reusable core sources, optionally add Writing use to an existing versioned literature note: original location, paragraph function, abstracted argument sequence, and applicability limits. Avoid storing copied paragraphs or creating a large generic template library. Follow existing project versioning and do not overwrite notes or prior drafts that lack reliable version history.
